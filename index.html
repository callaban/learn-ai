What this guide is

Modern AI is a stack, and almost nobody sees all of it. At the bottom is silicon patterned at atomic precision in a handful of factories on Earth. That silicon runs drivers and a GPU runtime. Above that, frameworks turn neural networks into computation graphs. Those graphs train on clusters of tens of thousands of accelerators drawing enough power to supply a city, then serve billions of requests a day under a completely different set of constraints. Above that, models are now given tools and told to get things done — in software, and increasingly in the physical world. Around all of it sits an unprecedented flow of capital and a scramble to write rules.

This guide covers every one of those layers properly, and connects them, because the interesting things happen where they meet.

Each page opens its ideas in plain language before going deep, so you can read at whatever level suits you. If you want a route through the material tailored to what you actually need — newcomer, engineer, builder or analyst — start with Learning Paths. If you would rather dive straight into the mathematics, begin with Neural Networks and follow the next-page links at the foot of each page.

Scope This guide covers the full stack: the mathematics, the systems engineering, the manufacturing, the applications, and the economics and governance that constrain all of them. It is deliberately technical, and deliberately opinionated where the evidence supports a position — but it flags contested questions as contested rather than pretending they are settled. Figures describing this industry are dated where it matters; treat forward-looking statements as informed projection.
