Understanding Artificial Intelligence

A deep technical guide to modern AI — from the mathematics of neural networks to the semiconductor foundries that fabricate the chips that power them. Written for readers who understand digital computing and want to understand AI at engineering depth.

Modern AI is a stack. At the bottom is silicon manufactured at sub-nanometre precision in a handful of foundries. That silicon runs firmware, drivers, and a CUDA/ROCm runtime. On top of that sit frameworks like PyTorch that express neural networks as computation graphs. Those graphs are trained on clusters of thousands of GPUs, consuming megawatts of power and petabytes of data. The trained model weights are then served at inference to millions of users, with a completely different optimization profile than training.

This site covers every layer of that stack in detail. The recommended reading order for a first pass is: Neural Networks → Training → LLMs → Inference → Hardware → Training Data Centers → Inference Data Centers → Foundry. The Glossary is a quick-reference companion for the whole guide.

Scope This guide focuses on the infrastructure and engineering side of AI — mathematics, hardware, systems, and manufacturing. AI applications, policy, and ethics are outside scope except where they directly constrain infrastructure design decisions.
